Koshari

Egypt's national dish

⏱ 45 min📊 Easy👤 4 servings

Koshari is the dish every Egyptian grows up on — a hearty, entirely vegetarian bowl of rice, lentils and macaroni, finished with crisp fried onions, chickpeas and a bright garlic-vinegar tomato sauce. It's sold from carts on nearly every Cairo street corner and made just as often at home.

How to Prepare

  1. 1

    Cook the rice

    Steam the rice until tender and fluffy, then set aside covered.

  2. 2

    Cook the lentils

    Simmer lentils in salted water until soft, about 20 minutes.

  3. 3

    Boil the macaroni

    Cook until just al dente, then drain.

  4. 4

    Fry the onions

    Fry sliced onions in oil until deep golden and crisp; drain on paper towels.

  5. 5

    Make the sauce

    Simmer tomato sauce with garlic vinegar and a pinch of chili until thickened.

  6. 6

    Layer & serve

    Layer rice, lentils, macaroni and chickpeas in a bowl, top with sauce and crispy onions.

Ingredient Details

7 ingredients

🍚Rice1 cupLong-grain, rinsed
Brown lentils1 cupPre-soaked 10 min
🍝Macaroni1 cupSmall elbow shape
🧅Onions3 largeThin half-moons
Chickpeas1 canDrained & warmed
🍅Tomato sauce2 cupsSimple passata

Flavor Profile

Savory
Spice
Aromatic
Richness

💡 Chef's Tip

Don't skip the garlic vinegar — it's the tang that ties every layer together. Make extra sauce; koshari always wants more.

👤Serves 4

Scale the rice and lentils 1:1 to feed a crowd.

Molokhia

Jute-leaf green stew

⏱ 1 hr 10 min📊 Medium👤 6 servings

Molokhia is a deep-green, garlicky stew made from finely chopped jute leaves simmered in rich broth. Ladled hot over rice, it's the dish most Egyptians name first when asked about home cooking.

How to Prepare

  1. 1

    Prepare the broth

    Bring chicken broth to a gentle simmer in a wide pot.

  2. 2

    Chop the leaves

    Finely chop or mince the molokhia leaves until almost fine as herbs.

  3. 3

    Make the taqliya

    Sizzle crushed garlic and coriander in hot ghee until fragrant, about 30 seconds.

  4. 4

    Combine

    Stir the taqliya and chopped leaves into the simmering broth.

  5. 5

    Simmer

    Cook gently for 10–15 minutes, stirring often — don't let it boil hard.

  6. 6

    Serve

    Ladle hot over steamed rice.

Ingredient Details

6 ingredients

🌿Molokhia500gFresh or frozen
🍗Broth4 cupsChicken or rabbit
🧄Garlic6 clovesCrushed, not minced
🌱Coriander2 tbspFreshly ground
🧈Ghee2 tbspOr clarified butter
🍚Rice2 cupsServed alongside

Flavor Profile

Savory
Spice
Aromatic
Richness

💡 Chef's Tip

The garlic-coriander taqliya is sizzled separately and stirred in last — it's what gives molokhia its signature aroma.

👤Serves 6

Pairs traditionally with plain rice and a squeeze of lemon.

Falafel

Egyptian fava bean falafel

⏱ 40 min📊 Medium👤 4 servings

Unlike the chickpea falafel found elsewhere, Egyptian ta'ameya is made from dried fava beans blended with a generous handful of fresh herbs, then rolled in sesame seeds before frying — giving it a shatteringly crisp shell and a vivid green center.

How to Prepare

  1. 1

    Soak the beans

    Soak split fava beans overnight in plenty of water.

  2. 2

    Grind with herbs

    Drain and process the beans with herbs, onion and garlic to a coarse paste.

  3. 3

    Season

    Mix in ground coriander, cumin, salt and a little baking powder; rest 20 minutes.

  4. 4

    Shape

    Form into small flat patties.

  5. 5

    Coat in sesame

    Press each patty into sesame seeds on both sides.

  6. 6

    Fry

    Deep fry in hot oil until deep golden and crisp, about 3 minutes per side.

Ingredient Details

6 ingredients

🫘Fava beans2 cupsDried, split, soaked
🌿Herbs2 cupsParsley & cilantro
🌱Dill½ cupFresh only
🧅Onion1 mediumRoughly chopped
🧄Garlic4 clovesPeeled
🌰Sesame seeds½ cupFor coating

Flavor Profile

Savory
Spice
Aromatic
Richness

💡 Chef's Tip

Never use canned or cooked beans — raw soaked fava is what gives ta'ameya its light, fluffy interior.

👤Serves 4

Makes about 20 patties — great tucked into warm bread.

Fattah

Layered rice & bread

⏱ 1 hr📊 Medium👤 6 servings

Fattah is a celebration dish — crisp toasted bread and fragrant rice soaked in a bright garlic-vinegar tomato sauce, layered and topped with tender slow-cooked beef. It's the centerpiece of feasts and family gatherings.

How to Prepare

  1. 1

    Toast the bread

    Tear bread into pieces and toast or fry until deeply crisp.

  2. 2

    Cook the rice

    Steam rice with a touch of ghee until fluffy.

  3. 3

    Boil the meat

    Simmer beef cubes with onion and bay leaf until fork-tender, about 1.5 hours.

  4. 4

    Make the sauce

    Combine tomato sauce with garlic vinegar and a splash of the beef broth; simmer.

  5. 5

    Layer

    Layer toasted bread, then rice, then soak generously with sauce.

  6. 6

    Top & serve

    Crown with the beef and a final drizzle of sauce.

Ingredient Details

6 ingredients

🍞Flatbread4 loavesToasted to shards
🍚Rice2 cupsLong-grain
🥩Beef1 kgChuck, cubed
🍅Tomato sauce2 cupsSimmered thick
🧄Garlic vinegar4 tbspSharp & bright
🧈Ghee3 tbspFor frying bread

Flavor Profile

Savory
Spice
Aromatic
Richness

💡 Chef's Tip

Soak the bread just before serving — any earlier and it turns soggy instead of staying pleasantly crisp underneath.

👤Serves 6

Traditionally served at celebrations and Eid gatherings.

Hawawshi

Egyptian spiced meat stuffed in bread

⏱ 35 min 📊 Easy 👤 4 servings

Hawawshi is a beloved Egyptian street food made by stuffing pita or baladi bread with seasoned minced beef, onion, peppers and aromatic spices. The stuffed bread is baked or grilled until the bread becomes crisp and golden while the meat remains juicy and flavorful.

How to Prepare

  1. 1

    Prepare the filling

    Mix the ground beef with chopped onion, green pepper, parsley, garlic, cumin, coriander, salt and black pepper.

  2. 2

    Prepare the bread

    Carefully open each loaf of baladi bread to create a pocket without tearing the bread.

  3. 3

    Stuff the bread

    Spread an even layer of the meat mixture inside each loaf. Do not overfill the bread.

  4. 4

    Brush with oil

    Lightly brush both sides of the stuffed bread with oil.

  5. 5

    Bake

    Bake in a preheated oven at 200°C until the bread is crisp and golden and the meat is fully cooked, about 15–20 minutes.

  6. 6

    Serve

    Cut the Hawawshi into pieces and serve hot with salad, pickles or tahini.

Ingredient Details

8 ingredients

🥩 Ground beef 500g Fresh minced beef
🧅 Onion 1 large Finely chopped
🌶️ Green pepper 1 medium Finely chopped
🌿 Parsley ½ cup Fresh & chopped
🧄 Garlic 3 cloves Minced
🌶️ Cumin 1 tsp Ground
🌿 Coriander 1 tsp Ground
🫓 Baladi bread 4 loaves Fresh Egyptian bread

Flavor Profile

Savory
Spice
Aromatic
Richness

💡 Chef's Tip

Spread the meat mixture evenly and keep it relatively thin. This helps the meat cook completely before the bread becomes too dark.

👤 Serves 4

Serve hot with pickles, salad, tahini or your favorite dipping sauce.

Mahshi

Stuffed zucchini & eggplant

⏱ 1 hr 15 min📊 Easy👤 6 servings

Egyptian Mahshi is a beloved homemade dish made with hollowed zucchini and eggplant stuffed with a flavorful rice mixture of tomatoes, onions, herbs and warm spices. The vegetables are slowly cooked in a rich tomato broth until tender, juicy and full of traditional Egyptian flavor..

How to Prepare

  1. 1

    Prepare the vegetables

    Wash the zucchini and eggplant. Carefully hollow them out using a vegetable corer, leaving enough flesh around the edges so they don't break.

  2. 2

    Make the rice filling

    Mix rinsed rice with grated tomatoes, chopped onion, parsley,cilantro, dill, garlic, oil, cumin, salt and pepper

  3. 3

    Stuff the zucchini & eggplant

    Fill each vegetable with the rice mixture, leaving about onequarter of the vegetable empty because the rice will expandwhile cooking.

  4. 4

    Arrange the pot

    Arrange the stuffed zucchini and eggplant tightly in a largecooking pot, standing them upright or laying them closely together.

  5. 5

    Add the tomato broth

    Mix tomato sauce with water, salt, pepper and a little cumin.Pour the liquid into the pot until it reaches about halfway upthe vegetables.

  6. 6

    Cook slowly

    Bring to a gentle boil, then reduce the heat, cover and simmerfor about 45–55 minutes until the vegetables are tender and therice is completely cooked.

  7. 7

    Serve

    Carefully remove the mahshi from the pot and serve hot withextra tomato sauce or a side of Egyptian salad.
